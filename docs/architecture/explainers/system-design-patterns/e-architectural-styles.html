The Quantum You Already Have

A style is a deployment-and-coupling bet — not a vendor, not a folder layout. You pick it by how many independently runnable parts you actually have, who owns the data, and what a wrong cut costs.

Audience Engineering & architecture Reading time ~13 min Status Explainer — catalog group E
How to read the colours: Verified mechanic Judgment / when-not Operator or product decision Uncertain in the source

Architects keep buying names. Microservices. Hexagonal. Serverless. The names sound like folders, vendors, or a slide title. Group E is thirteen Concepts that look like a menu and are actually one question asked four ways: how many independently runnable parts do you have, who owns the data, how do those parts collaborate, and what does a wrong cut cost?

A style is not a product you purchased, and it is not a directory tree. It is a deployment-and-coupling bet. The catalog’s name for the independently runnable part is the architecture quantum: the smallest unit that can run on its own. The database sits inside that unit. A shared store, or a synchronous call that inherits the callee’s availability, silently merges two hoped-for quanta back into one.

A style is a deployment-and-coupling bet, not a vendor or a folder layout. Pick by quantum, data ownership, and the cost of being wrong.

If you remember only that, you can retell Group E. The rest of this page is the four clusters that question sorts into — one deployable, independently deployable, collaboration overlay, integration hub — and the cuts the Concepts refuse to collapse.

THE LANDSCAPE

01Four clusters, not thirteen cards

Do not walk E1 through E13. The Concepts already tell you the sort key is the quantum, not the catalog number. One characteristics set and one shared store keep you in the monolith family. Own data and own release is how you leave. Events and read/write splits are overlays you can run on either side. A message hub is an integration topology, not an application style you start a product with.

Verified mechanic · the coupling test

Two things are coupled if changing one might break the other. The database is part of the quantum: a single shared store means a quantum of one, however many processes sit in front of it. After you pick sync or async, re-check — a blocking call on the user path lets checkout inherit payments’ availability, latency budget, and release nerve.

ONE TRAIN

02One deployable is a family, not a mess

A monolith is a deployment decision: one logical executable, one release train, one primary quantum. Internally it may be layers, domain modules, a hexagon, or a kernel with plug-ins. Those are partitioning choices. Cloned replicas behind a load balancer are still one quantum — same bits, same schema. A container image does not split it.

It is not a Big Ball of Mud. A BBOM is a failure of internal structure (Foote & Yoder). Shopify’s 2016 Rails estate — no boundaries, fragile changes, cascading tests — was a mud; the 2019 target was not. They rejected microservices and moved monolith → modular monolith: one application, strictly enforced domain boundaries. That is the family staying on one train on purpose.

Family versus partition

Monolith answers “one quantum?” Modular monolith answers “is that quantum cut on the domain, with published interfaces and enforced seams?” Recovered modular row: cost / simplicity / modularity HI; deploy / test 2★; scale / elasticity 1★; fault tolerance unsupported. Those stars belong to the enforced variant — not a folder tree, and not the family’s scorecard. The book has no star row labelled “Monolithic architecture.” If every type is public, layered / hexagonal / modular collapse to the same graph.

Who is the dependency sink?

Layered and hexagonal can share a drawing and a quantum and still be opposite arrows. Layered is horizontal technical bands — presentation / business / persistence / database — arrows top → bottom. The store is the sink. Hexagonal: ports are conversations, adapters are technology, the domain is the sink. Adding a “service layer” does not invert anything if business still compiles against the DAL. Folders labelled Clean whose arrows still run down onto a store are layered mechanics under a hexagonal name. Choose layered when change is technical. Choose hexagonal inside the same quantum when the domain must not depend on a store at compile time. That is not “add a layer.”

Kernel, roles, and what still does not add a quantum

A microkernel is still the family: stable core, independently developed plug-ins, a registry that names them. Recovered row: simplicity / cost HI; most other attributes 3★; scale / elasticity / fault tolerance LO. One Strategy selected by config is a modular seam, not a kernel. Remote plug-ins with their own store leave the family. Class-loader isolation is not process isolation.

Client–server is a style of roles, not a protocol and not a deployable count. One side initiates; the other listens, performs or rejects, and replies. The wire is request–response; bands inside a process are layered; how many server-side deployables you have is the family. Client–server owns who starts the exchange and where UI / rules / data sit. Typical quantum count is Uncertain — no style-selection row. Empty CRUD tiers are layered’s sinkhole wearing a hop: collapse the hop.

Judgment · when to stay, when to leave

Stay when you have one characteristics set, unknown bounded contexts, a team that can hold the system, cheap local ACID, or missing microservice prerequisites. Leave when counteracting sets fail the coupling test; when a named part must scale, fail, reside, or release independently; or when several teams are blocked on one train and fracture planes are known. Isolation is a reason to leave — the modular row records fault tolerance as unsupported. Fashion is not a fracture. Fowler: do not even consider microservices unless the system is already too complex as a monolith.

MANY TRAINS

03Independently deployable only if the data splits

The word “services” does not decide. Data ownership, not process count, is the discriminator.

Service-based (modern E6): separately deployed UI, usually ≤12 coarse domain services, usually one database. Processes deploy; the quantum rises only when a service gets its own persistence and you do not re-entangle it with a sync call. ≤12 is a service-count ceiling, not twelve quanta. Recovered differentiator: cost + simplicity — agility / test / deploy 4★, FT / availability 4★, scalability 3★, elasticity 2★, no 5★ anywhere. Best distributed style for ACID, because the transaction stays in one coarse service. Heavy chatter means the cut is wrong.

Orchestration-driven SOA (historical E6): taxonomy layers stitched by a smart bus. Residual fit is integration over someone else’s legacy. Recovered line: deploy / test “disastrous,” simplicity / cost inverted, quantum 1. Do not pick it for a new product because a governance page said “orchestration.”

Microservices need the invariants together: out-of-process service; independently deployable (coordinated releases fail); owns its state; business-capability boundaries; dumb pipes; failure as a design input. Size is not the discriminator. Star figures 1–5 did not survive; deploy / test are HI if independence holds; fault tolerance is HI only after the per-hop tax; scale is HI as a capability (Fowler 2015: no convincing “selective scale is cheaper” report); performance is “often an issue.” Most quanta of any style — a topology fact, not a headcount to chase.

Hosting is not a domain cut; cells are not a domain cut

Serverless is a hosting style: the vendor starts compute because an event or request arrived, and stops it when that unit ends. The unit of deployment is the function. The unit of domain is not. No serverless matrix row — the quantum is the function plus its managed store plus its sync callers. A queue trigger is an event-driven edge, not proof you adopted event-driven architecture. Long-running sync work and uniformly high utilization (FaaS can cost more than a box) are when-nots. Group E has no product timeout tables.

Cell-based asks how much of the fleet one poison tenant, bad deploy, or noisy neighbour can take. N complete-stack replicas plus a thin router and a control plane that places tenants. Shape: one design quantum, stamped N times, plus one shared router. A shared database collapses the costume. Cells partition load and fate; microservices partition domain and ownership. Picking one does not pick the other. Bulkheads live inside a cell. The router must stay thinner than the app. Well-Architected’s 1/N (10 cells → about 10% of keys) is still 100% for the tenants in the burning cell.

OVERLAYS

04Collaboration is a style; a topic is a mechanism

Event-driven architecture’s default collaboration is reaction to facts that already happened. It can be standalone or embedded — an in-process bus is still this style at quantum 1; processors with their own stores are many. Recovered row: evolvability 5★; performance / scale / fault tolerance 4★ (“4 not 5 because of the database”); simplicity / testability LO.

The two topologies are who owns the workflow, not which product you bought. Broker / choreography: no one owns multi-step state; unused events are legal. Mediator / orchestration: the mediator keeps state and can restart. A topic plus a process manager is mediator topology. A reply-queue over a broker is request–response wearing a queue. Pub/sub and outbox are mechanisms. An internal queue does not make a monolith event-driven. Processors that keep HTTP-GETting each other silently merge quanta while the diagram still shows a bus.

CQRS and event sourcing are persistence overlays inside one bounded context — not a first-class Richards/Ford style, and not event-driven architecture. CQRS is two objects where there was one; a second database is enabled, not required. Event sourcing makes the log the system of record. They are independent and often paired. Fowler 2017: CQRS “isn’t really about events”; event sourcing “need not be asynchronous.” People who blamed “event sourcing” for updating two models were usually doing CQRS. Same store first. Fowler found the majority of CQRS cases he had seen were harmful.

THE HUB

05A hub brokers messages, not IP packets

Hub-and-spoke, here, is the enterprise integration hub: an active Message Broker that replaces a fully-connected application graph. Hohpe: a wheel has n edges; the undirected mesh has n/2 · (n−1) — 4 apps → 6, 8 → 28. The lasting benefit is location transparency that is real only with protocol translation. Without a canonical model, n-square returns at format.

The same words appear on Azure VNet and AWS Transit Gateway diagrams. That is a lookalike. Integration nodes are applications; spoke-to-spoke traffic always goes via the hub; hub failure stops integration. Network nodes are VNets; peering is nontransitive; a spoke may still run locally. Do not migrate an integration problem by adding a Transit Gateway, or a network problem by buying an ESB. It is also not an API gateway, and not SOA-by-purchase. Hohpe wants a canonical model and stateless scale-out; Mason’s EAI hub usually maps formats directly and parks state in a shared DB — easy PoC, SPOF. Averaging them hides the decision. No style-selection row: do not invent stars or “typical = 1 + N spokes.”

THE COST OF BEING WRONG

06Migration is cheap only in one direction

Inside the family, the default first move is impose seams, stay on one deployable. Shopify rejected microservices and moved monolith → modular monolith (2020: 2.8M lines, 37 components). Isolating “Platform Essentials” still took months after a checker’s todos hit zero. Fowler had few success stories of designing as if services while staying in-process.

The priced exit is coarse services, often still one database — service-based, not a leap to microservices. Extract after prerequisites and a named fracture. Split that service’s data or the quantum count does not change. SnapCI knew CI, still got boundaries wrong, and merged back. Merging is cheaper than inventing a distributed transaction. The day you leave one process you pay the fallacies (network, latency, versioning, compensating updates, observability). A fashion extract buys them before you have a fracture that pays for them.

This workspace’s receipts

The style-decision (2026-07-26) and ADR 0005 picked one quantum: one Spring Boot deployable, three domain modules (conversion, validation-certification, evidence), one primary datastore. Layered as macro was rejected — change streams are domain clusters. Microservices lost on semantic coupling plus the fashion check. Service-based was the strongest distributed candidate and lost because Determination 1 found no quantum boundary: the IR spine is shared; Preserve Provenance has CA = 13 of 16; a synchronous Certify→Models call would collapse a split. Replay-on-devices and cluster C looked extractable and failed — shared lineage store; audit writes that must share a local transaction.

Named destination: service-based, when a named flip appears (residency; a second live execution backend reopens a localized SPI). CI asserts exactly one deployable. A microkernel registry was declined (Volatile Core on conversion). Orchestration-driven SOA was rejected in one line. Client–server is the perimeter — operators initiate; the process reacts. Hexagonal is available inside a cluster, not as top-level packages.

Judgment · the cost of the wrong cut

Wrong modular seams are a refactor. Wrong service seams are a network migration. A distributed monolith — HTTP plus shared schema plus lock-step release — pays both taxes and keeps one quantum. Grains of sand, saga sprawl, a gateway that owns the workflow, dual-write onto a bus, and a shared-DB “cell” are the same mistake in different clothes: you advertised many quanta and shipped one.

08Cells the Concepts left empty

Everything above this line is a claim a Group E Concept actually makes. What follows is parked so a review cannot “complete” the matrix.

◆ Uncertain · typical quantum

Client–server has no style-selection row. Typical quantum count is Uncertain. There is no star scorecard. Do not invent “2+.” Azure tiers are scalability / security / reliability boundaries, not automatically new quanta. Whether an org-unowned browser or a BFF is a quantum is left open. This workspace’s “one server-side quantum” is a local pick, not a catalog FACT.

◆ Uncertain · no matrix row

Hexagonal, hub-and-spoke, serverless, and CQRS/event sourcing have no Richards/Ford matrix row — no typical-quanta cell, no stars. An absent row is not a 3★. Cell-based likewise has no prose-recovered stars; the “1 design quantum × N copies” shape is Well-Architected plus quantum machinery, not a book rating. “Typical hub = 1 + N spokes” is explicitly not a catalog FACT.

◆ Uncertain · unrecovered stars

Microservices star figures 1–5 did not survive into the workspace notes; simplicity / cost stays —. Layered’s FSA chapter-10 scorecard is —. Richards 2015 High/Low prose is a different book — do not merge it with later stars. Orchestration-SOA and service-based have recovered cells only where style-selection recorded them; other cells stay —.

◆ Uncertain · unsettled arguments

Start-distributed versus monolith-first remains unsettled as a law — Fowler’s 2015 observation (greenfield-as-microservices ended in trouble) is recorded; do not treat MonolithFirst as a statute. IBM’s “faster because teams per tier” contradicts Fowler’s vote against layer-teams — contested, not a law. Accidental SOA and shared-entity-library mechanics are name-checks; the source file that would define them is missing. Exact FSA sentences for Volatile Core and Plug-In Dependencies were unrecovered on the research fetch.

A style is a deployment-and-coupling bet. Count the quanta you actually have — store included — and price the cut you cannot cheaply undo.

If you can say that without opening a Concept, Group E did its job. The thirteen cards are the receipts, not the menu.
