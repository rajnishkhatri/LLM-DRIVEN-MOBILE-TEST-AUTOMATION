# When standards fall short

**See also:** [TOGAF](togaf.md) · [standardisation](standardisation.md) · [enterprise architecture](enterprise-architecture.md)

The book will keep citing standards. They are not always useful. The chapter’s worked example is TOGAF itself.

Criticisms the source collects (footnote: taken from the Wikipedia page on TOGAF):

- Lacks concrete examples and use cases; hard to apply in the real world.
- Impractical to follow step-by-step; prescriptions are vague and need adaptation without clear guidance.
- Studies show TOGAF-based EA practice often **diverges** from the original framework.
- Jason Bloomberg: adoption is driven more by a **lack of alternatives** than by utility.
- v9.2 and the 10th Edition did not fix a rigid planning approach; some treat popularity as promotion, not value.

This is the same tension as [financial messaging standards](standardisation.md): a published core drifts the moment products and organisations need something the standard does not have.

## Solution architecture, briefly

After leaving EA, the chapter names **solution architecture** (one problem, one design) as distinct from the enterprise frame. See [enterprise architecture](enterprise-architecture.md#solution-architecture).

**Architect takeaway:** steal TOGAF’s *vocabulary* (domains, continuum, “architecture description”) and drop the parts you cannot staff. A half-finished ADM cycle that nobody can map to a payment rail is worse than a one-page [component map](architecture-components.md) that the engineers actually use.
