# The Tree Card

Build the tree before you open your mouth. One private rep a day, and the same three beats any time someone asks you to explain something.

Memorize only: the governing sentence, the three beat labels, and the frame line.

**Governing sentence:** "Build the tree before you open your mouth."

**Frame line:** "Give me a second. I want to frame this clearly."

## Before you speak

1. **Trunk.** The answer in one sentence, before any history. That sentence is the trunk. Context is a branch, and it comes second. If the sentence will not form, say the frame line, then the sentence.
2. **Count.** "There are two reasons." The number forces the edit. Three is the ceiling. A listener can hold a small container.
3. **Stop.** Say those branches and stop. Use the plain word. If the precise word is late, leave the pause. The pause is the search.

## Once a day, four minutes, alone

Pick one idea from the day: something you read, a decision, or a thought that is only half built. Say the trunk, the number, and only those branches out loud. Then write the sentence you actually said, and which trap showed up.

The first attempt comes out as a draft. That is the rep. The blank at the start is the unfinished thought. Speak anyway.

One restart is part of the rep. If the history starts, stop and open again on the trunk.

## When it comes out scrambled

Name the trap after a live miss, then write the trunk you wish you had opened with. One miss, one correction.

| Trap | What you did | Correction |
|---|---|---|
| Bigger word | Swapped a plain verb for a longer one | Say the plain word. The longer one added no information. |
| Backstory first | Started where the timeline started | Answer the question in the first sentence. |
| Whole web | Touched several ideas and landed none | Announce the number, then stop at that number. |
| Panic at silence | Filled the half-second with a nearby word | Wait. The pause sits in front of the precise word. |
| Unfinished thought | Asked your mouth to finish a half-built idea | Take the second, or build it in private before the room. |

## Thesis from the first reps

An idea becomes clear in two passes.

1. **Say the draft.** The first time an idea comes out, it is a draft. Saying it out loud shows the gaps, and more points show up as you keep thinking. Those points stay unfinished until you say them or write them. The blank at the start is part of it. The practice is what gives them structure.
2. **Edit.** After the first draft, you become the editor. The points you already have are the structure, and the gaps are what you research or sit with. Then you sort. A few points carry the signal. The redundant ones go. What remains is the thesis.

**Say this once:** "An idea becomes clear in two passes. There are two passes. First, say the draft out loud so the gaps show up. Second, edit: work those gaps, keep the high-signal points, and discard the rest. What remains is the thesis."
