# Financial data architecture — bundle index

OKF bundle. Each entry is a typed Concept. See the convention in [CONVENTIONS.md](../../docs/CONVENTIONS.md).

- [Access layer](access-layer.md) — How people and systems reach financial data: dashboards, reports, APIs, cloud shares, semantic layers — plus access control for who, what, and when.
- [AI adoption in financial data](ai-adoption.md) — Models need governed, task-relevant data. Generative tools can also extract entities from messy text. Quality and governance decide whether AI helps or hallucinates.
- [Components of a financial data architecture](architecture-components.md) — Not one system: models, governance, integration, infrastructure, transformation, pipelines, access, and observability. Design and evaluate the set, not a single store.
- [Constraints and influences on financial data architectures](architecture-constraints.md) — Business needs, technology limits, cost, internal policy, and regulation bound the blueprint. Ignore them and the design is a drawing, not a building.
- [Architecture in IT](architecture-in-it.md) — ISO/IEC/IEEE 42010: architecture is the fundamental concepts and properties of an entity in its environment — elements, relationships, behaviour, and governing principles.
- [Data architecture maturity](architecture-maturity.md) — Five levels: Initial/Ad Hoc, Repeatable, Defined, Managed, Optimized. Score by how consistently data is structured, governed, integrated, and used — not by a vanity number.
- [Categorising financial data architectures](classifying-architectures.md) — Six lenses: storage, deployment, modeling, processing, business function, and ownership. They overlap. This book leads with business function and domain problems.
- [Characteristics of financial data](data-characteristics.md) — Wide, hierarchical, semantic, many-identified, and fast-changing. The hard part is meaning and linkage, not row count.
- [Financial data arrival and exchange](data-delivery.md) — APIs, feeds, webhooks, files, terminals, queues, cloud shares, and ledgers are different contracts. Pick the mechanism for latency, volume, and control — not habit.
- [The financial data ecosystem](data-ecosystem.md) — A financial system’s complexity is bounded by the landscape it maps: generation, sources, delivery, types, characteristics, and the value chain.
- [Financial data generation mechanisms](data-generation.md) — Markets generate data from trading, payments, issuance, corporate actions, lending, filings, and more. One equity buy leaves records on broker, venue, and clearing systems.
- [Data quality and integrity](data-quality.md) — Inaccurate or late data causes bad decisions, failed transactions, missed risk, and losses. Quality is accuracy, completeness, timeliness, and consistency — each with its own work.
- [Data silos and fragmentation](data-silos.md) — Client, transaction, and risk data live in different systems. Silos block a single source of truth and produce conflicting pictures of the same client.
- [Financial data sources](data-sources.md) — Exchanges, OTC venues, regulators, vendors, institutions, FMIs, and alternative data each own a different slice. There is no single central repository.
- [Financial data types, formats, and structures](data-types-formats.md) — Structure is how you organise (series, panel, graph). Type is what it means (market, reference, transaction). Format is how it travels (CSV, FIX, Parquet).
- [Defining financial data architecture](defining-financial-data-architecture.md) — A domain-specific blueprint for collecting, storing, sharing, consuming, and integrating financial data — traditional data architecture plus the Chapter 1 ecosystem and market governance.
- [Enterprise and solution architecture](enterprise-architecture.md) — EA designs the organisation’s IT, processes, and data toward strategy. Solution architecture delivers one problem. TOGAF is the common EA method; it is not the only framework.
- [Financial data architectures](fin-data-arch.md) — Architecture is the fundamental concepts of an entity in its environment. Financial data architecture is the domain-specific blueprint for collecting, storing, sharing, and governing that data.
- [Finance basics](finance-basics.md) — Asset, instrument, and security are nested terms. An order is an instruction, a trade is the exchange, a transaction is the record. Clearing confirms; settlement transfers.
- [Financial data governance](financial-data-governance.md) — Policies, roles, processes, and tech for quality, security, integrity, and compliance across the lifecycle — constraints, lineage, catalogs, and contracts, not only access tickets.
- [Financial data models](financial-data-models.md) — Models define accounts, instruments, transactions, customers, and events — and how they relate. Conceptual, logical, and physical layers give business and systems a shared language.
- [Data integration and aggregation](integration-aggregation.md) — Integration joins formats, identifiers, and entities across sources. Aggregation rolls the firm up for risk, customer view, regulation, and fraud.
- [Integration and interfaces](integration-interfaces.md) — In, out, and across: vendor ingest, partner and regulator share, and internal exchange. APIs, virtualisation, ISO 20022/FIX, queues, and files are the mechanisms — the interface is the contract.
- [An introduction to financial data](introduction.md) — Finance is an information industry. Data is the core of how market participants operate, not a byproduct. Architecture is how you manage that complexity.
- [Legacy systems](legacy-systems.md) — Mainframes and COBOL still clear high-volume payments. They are reliable and siloed. Migration is costly; not everything should move.
- [Management vs architecture vs engineering](management-architecture-engineering.md) — Management sets the objective. Architecture is the blueprint. Engineering builds and runs the infrastructure. Do not hire one title and expect all three.
- [Data management challenges in financial services](management-challenges.md) — Silos, drifting standards, reference data, legacy cores, quality, privacy, integration, scale, regulation, and AI all persist across firms. Architecture is the response.
- [Monitoring and observability](monitoring-observability.md) — Not set-and-forget. Watch pipelines and stores for failures; watch quality metrics for bad records. Observability is itself a data problem: metrics, events, logs, traces.
- [Pipelines and orchestration](pipelines-orchestration.md) — Pipelines are the sequenced tasks of ingest, transform, deliver. Orchestration adds dependencies, retries, alerts, audit, and batch-or-stream — often several tools, not one.
- [Processing and transformation logic](processing-transformation.md) — Business-driven conversion of raw data into usable data: validate, clean, harmonise, enrich, transform — rounding, internal IDs, and metric calculations included.
- [Reference data management](reference-data.md) — Slow-changing identifiers, terms, and classifications underpin every trade and report. They are often extracted from prose and must be linked across identifier schemes.
- [Regulatory compliance as a data problem](regulatory-compliance.md) — Market rules are data-architecture requirements: aggregation, lineage, auditability, privacy, and a file you can produce in 72 hours. Fragmented regimes make rigidity fatal.
- [Scalability and performance](scalability-performance.md) — Population, cards, cross-border, and instant-payment rules raise volume. Speed without weaker AML is the trade-off, not a free upgrade.
- [Data security and privacy](security-privacy.md) — Financial data reveals behaviour and vulnerability. Cloud, open banking, and privacy law make protection a design constraint, not a perimeter product.
- [Financial data standardisation](standardisation.md) — Standards lower cost until products and regs force proprietary extensions. Then “standard” messages are standard in name only, and the cycle restarts.
- [When standards fall short](standards-criticism.md) — TOGAF is the de facto EA standard and is still criticised as vague, hard to apply, and popular for lack of alternatives. Use standards as a vocabulary, not a script.
- [Technological infrastructure](technological-infrastructure.md) — Storage follows use case: SQL for OLTP, warehouse for analytics, lake for raw and ML I/O, kdb+ for HFT time series. Processing is stream or batch, on-prem, cloud, or managed.
- [TOGAF — domains, ADM, and the continuum](togaf.md) — TOGAF adds a component-and-principles definition to ISO 42010. Five layers in Figure 2-1 (business, data, application, technology, security at the base). ADM is the lifecycle. The continuum refines generic to firm-specific.
- [The financial data value chain](value-chain.md) — Production creates the raw signal. Distribution standardises it. Augmentation turns it into insight. Embedding puts it inside a product.
- [When a financial data architecture is not needed](when-not-needed.md) — Skip a heavy design when data is simple, the effort is a short project, or a third-party platform already is the architecture. Growth and regulation usually end that exemption.
- [Why master financial data architecture](why-master.md) — Treat it as a skill, not only a title. In finance, data is the product. Domain design, AI-era framing, market demand, and innovation all sit on the blueprint.
- [Why a financial data architecture is needed](why-needed.md) — Fragmented systems, identifiers, legacy, silos, and AI need a structure so sharing, onboarding, reporting, and quality have a standard answer instead of a project.
