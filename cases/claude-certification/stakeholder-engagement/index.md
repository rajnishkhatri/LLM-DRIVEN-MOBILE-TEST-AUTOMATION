# Claude Architect — stakeholder engagement — bundle index

OKF bundle. Each entry is a typed Concept. See the convention in [CONVENTIONS.md](../../../docs/CONVENTIONS.md).

- [Capstone: architect a regulated multi-platform deployment end to end](cumulative.md) — Seven sequenced decisions for a two-state clinical-documentation assistant: discovery row, logging-versus-latency tradeoff, scheduled audit trigger, residency decision log, Bedrock plus API map, CFO outcome metrics, expansion gate.
- [Watch-out: the discovery call that turned into a design session](discovery-became-design.md) — Failure trace: two stakeholder statements produced a plausible sketch. Licensed authorization, PHI handling, and two-state retention never got asked, and they surfaced two weeks later in compliance review.
- [A discovery call is a structured elicitation, not a conversation](discovery.md) — Listen, translate, write down. Four question buckets turn a preference such as seamless into a testable constraint, then one translation-table row per item keeps the reasoning intact.
- [Documentation that survives your absence serves the handoff recipient, the auditor, and the returning Architect](documentation-handoff.md) — One document, three readers. Completeness is whether a competent Architect who was not in the room can make a safe change after reading it: decision, rejected alternatives, named tradeoff, owner, evidence.
- [Entry-point selection returns with the full production picture, and the outcome document turns the work into reusable IP](entry-point-outcomes.md) — Live multi-platform routing needs an entry-point responsibility map. An outcome document is six fields: scope, metric before, metric after, auditable control, measurement owner, reuse potential.
- [The feedback loop decides which signals reach a stakeholder, and the SLA names what you owe when one breaks](feedback-loop.md) — A feedback loop is the decision layer above observability: signal, trigger, owner, action. SLA thresholds trace to UX, criticality, or evals. Regulated checkpoints fire on a schedule, not only at a threshold.
- [Glossary](glossary.md) — Module 4 terms from control register through translation table: reversal cost, feedback loop, outcome document, SLA, scenario-specific demo, and related lifecycle vocabulary.
- [Watch-out: the observability stack that replaced the feedback loop](observability-replaced-loop.md) — Failure trace: eval score drifted from week four with no hard alert. Nothing mapped slow quality drift to a review trigger, so the stakeholder noticed in week twelve.
- [Module introduction: stakeholder engagement, lifecycle, and go-to-market](orientation.md) — Module 4 intro. Five outcomes: structured discovery, tradeoff framing, a feedback-loop governance table, handoff documentation, and an outcome document a sponsor can take to a CFO.
- [Watch-out: the design rationale that lived in the Architect's head](rationale-in-head.md) — Failure trace: a thorough diagram with no rejected alternatives. The successor switched context strategy to fix latency and reintroduced the residency violation the original design had solved.
- [Checkpoint: recommend the option and name the missing element](recommend-the-option.md) — Recommend the logging-in option for a regulated high-volume insurer. Option B is missing reversal cost; option C violates the audit-trail constraint.
- [Recap: five things that hold across everything here](takeaways.md) — Translate preferences into constraints, name reversal cost, wire signals to owners, write rejected alternatives while you still hold them, and capture the business metric a CFO can defend.
- [Present a tradeoff the stakeholder can act on, and design a demo that moves the deal](tradeoff-framing.md) — Frame every choice as gain, give-up, and reversal cost. A capabilities demo creates interest; only a scenario-specific demo creates confidence. Partner-track demo, scoping, and objections sit on the same structure.
- [Checkpoint: find the undocumented assumption](undocumented-assumption.md) — Three requirements trace to something the stakeholder said; sixty-day transcript retention does not. An unsourced assumption is the item that nobody remembers deciding.
- [Watch-out: the approval that was not an informed choice](uninformed-approval.md) — Failure trace: the CTO approved four cents per call. Nobody translated that to monthly spend or named reversal cost, so a five-figure invoice arrived as a surprise.
- [Watch-out: the outcome document that measured the wrong thing](wrong-metric-outcome.md) — Failure trace: volume, latency, and error rate proved the system ran. The CFO asked what claim-processing time did before and after, and the document could not answer.
