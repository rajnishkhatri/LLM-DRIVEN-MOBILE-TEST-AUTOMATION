## 1. Canon and definitions

**Nygard, *Release It!*** — 1st ed. Pragmatic Bookshelf 2007 (ISBN 9780978739218); 2nd ed. January 2018 (ISBN 9781680502398). Circuit Breaker is the second of the 2nd edition's twelve **Stability Patterns**: Timeouts, Circuit Breaker, Bulkheads, Steady State, Fail Fast, Let It Crash, Handshaking, Test Harnesses, Decoupling Middleware, Shed Load, Create Back Pressure, Governor. The twelve **Stability Antipatterns** it answers: Integration Points, Chain Reactions, Cascading Failures, Users, Blocked Threads, Self-Denial Attacks, Scaling Effects, Unbalanced Capacities, Dogpile, Force Multiplier, Slow Responses, Unbounded Result Sets. Versus the 1st edition (InfoQ review, 2007-12-20): added Dogpile, Force Multiplier, Let It Crash, Shed Load, Create Back Pressure, Governor; dropped SLA Inversion. The book's per-pattern mapping of breaker → antipatterns was not fetchable (§7).

**Fowler, "CircuitBreaker" (2014-03-06).** Wrap the protected call in an object that counts failures and trips at a threshold; a software breaker must self-reset via a trial call after an interval (the half-open state); **not every error should trip it** — connection failures and timeouts yes, ordinary application outcomes no; richer tripping uses error *frequency* (e.g. 50%) and per-error-type thresholds (his example: 10 timeouts, 3 connection failures); breakers apply to **asynchronous** integration too (trip when the outbound queue fills); every state change should be logged and exposed, and operators should be able to trip or reset manually; the tripped breaker also relieves the struggling callee. The post says nothing about sharing breaker state across processes.

**Azure Architecture Center, "Circuit Breaker pattern"** (ms.date 2025-02-05, updated 2026-07-02). Fourteen "Problems and considerations": exception handling; types of exceptions (weight them — e.g. require more timeouts than hard-unavailable errors); monitoring of failed *and* successful calls with tracing; recoverability (open duration matched to the dependency's recovery; too long = needless errors, too short = fluctuation); failed-operation testing (ping or a health operation instead of a pure timer); manual override (force-close, force-open); concurrency (no blocking, low per-call overhead); **resource differentiation** (one breaker over many shards blocks healthy shards or admits doomed calls); **accelerated circuit breaking** (a 429/503 with an expected delay justifies opening immediately for at least that long); multiregion deployments; service-mesh breakers as sidecars; failed-request replay (journal while open); **inappropriate time-outs on external services** (a long dependency timeout pins threads before the breaker learns anything); adaptability to compute diversification (cold starts). "Not suitable": local in-process resources; a substitute for business-logic exception handling; when plain retries suffice; when waiting for reset is unacceptable; message-driven systems that already dead-letter and retry; when the platform (LB health checks, mesh) already handles recovery. Related patterns linked: Retry, Health Endpoint Monitoring only. Companion "Retry Storm antipattern" (ms.date 2025-07-16) prescribes the breaker specifically against retry storms.

**Google SRE book (2016).** Ch. 21 *Handling Overload*: client-side adaptive throttling rejects locally with probability `max(0, (requests − K·accepts) / (requests + 1))` over a two-minute window, K = 2 by default; per-request cap of three attempts; per-client **retry budget of 10%**; an attempt counter in request metadata lets backends answer "overloaded, don't retry"; retry only at the layer immediately above the rejecting one. The chapter never uses the words "circuit breaker" — its mechanisms are probabilistic, not binary. Ch. 22 *Addressing Cascading Failures*: **retry amplification** (three retries at three layers = 4³ = 64 attempts on the database); randomized exponential backoff; a server-wide retry budget (e.g. 60/min per process); a caution that load balancers which avoid error-serving backends shrink capacity and snowball load; deadline propagation (30 s → 23 s → 19 s). SRE Workbook ch. 11: Pokémon GO launch at ~50× forecast, synchronized client retries producing 20× peaks; fix = jitter + truncated exponential backoff + admin rate limits.

**AWS Builders' Library and Marc Brooker** (originals now redirect to `builder.aws.com`; verified from archived copies). *Timeouts, retries, and backoff with jitter* (Brooker): pick timeouts from the latency distribution (p99.9 for a 0.1% false-timeout budget); retries are "selfish"; five layers × three retries = 243× database load; **breakers add modal behaviour that is hard to test and can lengthen recovery** — Amazon's alternative is a local retry **token bucket**, in the AWS SDK since 2016. *Avoiding fallback in distributed systems* (Gabrielson): the ~2001 shipping-speed cache whose "query the database if the cache is down" fallback turned a partial outage into a total one; fallback paths carry latent bugs and are exercised only when things are already breaking; prefer making the primary reliable, pushing data proactively, or continuously exercised failover. *Using load shedding to avoid overload* (Yanacek): goodput over throughput; shed early and cheaply; prioritize health checks and in-flight work; deadline propagation; LIFO queues where allowed. Brooker's blog: *Will circuit breakers solve my problems?* (2022-02-16) — a client-side breaker over a sharded backend sees a blend and either never trips or takes healthy shards offline; breakers risk converting partial failure into total failure. *Fixing retries with token buckets and circuit breakers* (2022-02-28): 100-client simulation; a breaker applied only to *retries* (first attempts always pass) controls load but is modal and noisy per client. *What is Backoff For?* (2022-08-11): backoff helps closed-loop systems and short spikes, not sustained open-loop overload. *Metastability and Distributed Systems* (2021-05-24). Exponential backoff and jitter (2015-03-04): Full Jitter `sleep = random(0, min(cap, base·2^attempt))` minimizes total calls.

**Metastable-failure literature.** HotOS '21 (Bronson, Aghayev, Charapko, Zhu): stable → vulnerable → metastable; a *trigger* plus a *sustaining effect* (usually work amplification) — the loop, not the trigger, is the root cause; a database good for 300 QPS with one retry is vulnerable above 150 QPS; a look-aside cache losing a 90% hit rate yields 10× query amplification and cannot refill. OSDI '22 (Huang et al., *Metastable Failures in the Wild*): 22 incidents from 11 organizations; retry policy is the most common sustaining effect (> 50%); outages 1.5–73.5 h; > 55% recovered by direct load shedding or throttling. HotOS '25 (Isaacs et al., *Analyzing Metastable Failures*): queueing-model ensembles applied at AWS. Incidents: AWS DynamoDB us-east-1 2015-09-20 (membership-data storm; recovery required pausing metadata requests); AWS Kinesis 2020-11-25 (OS thread limit; hundreds of server restarts per hour); Slack 2022-02-22 (cache loss → database overload → cache cannot refill; recovery by a client-boot throttle raised in small steps); GitHub 2025-08-12 (retry queues overwhelmed load balancers); Cloudflare 2026-02-20 (added a deployment "circuit breaker" that halts rollouts on health signals).

**Industry tuning posts.** Shopify *Your Circuit Breaker is Misconfigured* (2020-02-18): a slightly wrong breaker is as bad as none; keep `error_threshold` small, `error_timeout` large relative to the half-open probe timeout, and the probe timeout far below the service timeout; one worked tuning cut required spare capacity from 263% to 4%. Eskildsen napkin math (2020-08): a 5 s timeout on one dependency turns 100 ms workers into 5 s workers (~1/50 capacity); a 3-error / 15 s breaker still leaves ~50% capacity. Yandex Go *Good Retry, Bad Retry* (2024-08-09): 9× load at the callee vs 3× at its caller — retries; a request-level breaker at a 10% threshold **flapped** when one of five shards failed; fix = a 10% retry budget (local token bucket), breakers kept as a complement. Netflix *Performance Under Load* (2018-03-23): adaptive concurrency limits replace static thresholds; *Service-Level Prioritized Load Shedding* (2024-06-24): partitioned limiter kept user-initiated availability above 99.4% during a 12× prefetch spike. Uber Cinnamon (2023-11-22): PID controller + modified TCP-Vegas limit, 768 priorities, ~1 µs overhead. Slack CI/CD breakers (2022-08-19): orchestration-level, two states only, driven by Prometheus queries. Cloudflare AI code review (2026-04-20): Hystrix-inspired per-model-tier breakers open on 429/503, wait two minutes, admit one probe, fail back to older model generations.

**Resilience4j specifics.** Defaults: `failureRateThreshold` 50, `slowCallRateThreshold` 100, `slowCallDurationThreshold` 60000 ms, `permittedNumberOfCallsInHalfOpenState` 10, `maxWaitDurationInHalfOpenState` 0, `slidingWindowType` COUNT_BASED, `slidingWindowSize` 100, `minimumNumberOfCalls` 100, `waitDurationInOpenState` 60000 ms, `automaticTransitionFromOpenToHalfOpenEnabled` false, `recordExceptions` / `ignoreExceptions` empty (ignore takes precedence; enforced in 2.4.0), `writableStackTraceEnabled` true; 2.4.0 adds `transitionToStateAfterWaitDuration` (may be CLOSED to skip half-open) and `initialState`. Rates are only evaluated once `minimumNumberOfCalls` outcomes are in the window (nine failures out of nine calls do not trip with a minimum of 10). OPEN → HALF_OPEN happens lazily on the next call unless the automatic transition is enabled. Special states: `METRICS_ONLY` (admits all, records, never trips), `DISABLED` (admits all, no events), `FORCED_OPEN` (rejects all). Events: onSuccess, onError, onIgnoredError, onReset, onStateTransition, onCallNotPermitted, onFailureRateExceeded, onSlowCallRateExceeded. Micrometer: `resilience4j.circuitbreaker.calls` (timer, `kind` = successful/failed/ignored), `.not.permitted.calls`, `.state` (gauge per state), `.buffered.calls`, `.slow.calls`, `.failure.rate`, `.slow.call.rate`. Spring aspect order: `Retry( CircuitBreaker( RateLimiter( TimeLimiter( Bulkhead( fn )))))` — Retry outermost. Actuator: `/actuator/circuitbreakers`, `/actuator/circuitbreakerevents`, and a write operation accepting CLOSE / FORCE_OPEN / DISABLE.

**Polly v8 specifics.** `ShouldHandle` defaults to any exception except `OperationCanceledException`; states Closed / Open / HalfOpen / **Isolated**; `BrokenCircuitException` and `IsolatedCircuitException` expose `RetryAfter`. Documented anti-patterns: varying retry sleep by breaker state; one breaker per endpoint misuse; inspecting state to avoid exceptions. **.NET standard resilience handler** (`Microsoft.Extensions.Http.Resilience` 10.10.0, 2026-09-09), outermost → innermost: rate limiter (1000 permits, queue 0) → total request timeout 30 s → retry (3 retries, exponential + jitter, 2 s base) → circuit breaker (ratio 10%, min throughput 100, sampling 30 s, break 5 s) → attempt timeout 10 s; failures = HTTP ≥ 500, 408, 429, `HttpRequestException`, `TimeoutRejectedException`; `DisableForUnsafeHttpMethods()` turns retries off for POST/PATCH/PUT/DELETE.

**Hystrix and successors.** Trip requires volume ≥ `requestVolumeThreshold` **and** error % > threshold; thread-pool isolation (per-dependency bounded pool with timeout) vs semaphore isolation (counter, no timeout); `HystrixBadRequestException` is not counted. Maintenance mode since README commit 2018-11-19; Netflix recommends Resilience4j and adaptive concurrency limits. **Netflix/concurrency-limits 0.5.4** (2025-12-08): Little's law (limit ≈ RPS × latency); Vegas (delay-based, α = 3·log10 L, β = 6·log10 L), Gradient (`gradient = tolerance × rttNoLoad / rtt`), Gradient2 (long-window EMA baseline, gradient clamped to [0.5, 1.0]), AIMD (+1 per window, ×0.9 on drop); integrations for gRPC and servlets (429).

## 3. Infrastructure-level breakers

**Two different things share the name.** Envoy's *circuit breaking* is a set of per-cluster **concurrency limits** (`max_connections` 1024, `max_pending_requests` 1024, `max_requests` 1024, `max_retries` 3, optional `retry_budget` of 20% of active + pending requests with `min_retry_concurrency` 3, which overrides `max_retries` when set); overflow increments `upstream_cx_overflow`, `upstream_rq_pending_overflow`, `upstream_rq_active_overflow`, `upstream_rq_retry_overflow`, and the router sets `x-envoy-overloaded`. Envoy's *outlier detection* is the failure-trip breaker: `consecutive_5xx` 5 (enforced 100%), `consecutive_gateway_failure` 5 (502/503/504; enforced 0% by default), `interval` 10 s, `base_ejection_time` 30 s, `max_ejection_time` 300 s, `max_ejection_time_jitter` 0 s, `max_ejection_percent` 10%, success-rate ejection (`minimum_hosts` 5, `request_volume` 100, `stdev_factor` 1900 → mean − 1.9 σ), failure-percentage ejection (threshold 85, enforced 0%). Ejection time = base × consecutive ejection count, capped; the multiplier decays while healthy; a passing active health check un-ejects by default. Ejected hosts are skipped unless the balancer enters **panic mode** (`healthy_panic_threshold` 50%: spread across all hosts or fail all; `lb_healthy_panic` stat). gRPC statuses map to HTTP for bookkeeping (UNAVAILABLE → 503, DEADLINE_EXCEEDED → 504, INTERNAL/UNKNOWN/DATA_LOSS → 500, RESOURCE_EXHAUSTED → 429, INVALID_ARGUMENT → 400, NOT_FOUND → 404, CANCELLED → 499); only 5xx-mapped codes feed `consecutive_5xx`. Envoy's **adaptive concurrency filter** (`gradient = (minRTT + 25% buffer) / sampleRTT`, `new_limit = gradient × old_limit + √limit`, `max_concurrency_limit` 1000, minRTT re-sampled at `min_concurrency` 3 over 50 requests, rejects with 503) is the latency-driven alternative. All Envoy state is **per proxy**, uncoordinated.

**Istio 1.31** `DestinationRule`: `connectionPool` (Envoy thresholds; defaults 2³²−1, i.e. effectively unlimited; `maxRequestsPerConnection` 0) and `outlierDetection` (`consecutive5xxErrors` 5, `consecutiveGatewayErrors` off, `interval` 10 s, `baseEjectionTime` 30 s × times ejected, `maxEjectionPercent` 10%, `minHealthPercent` 0% = panic surface disabled, `outlierDetectionHttpErrorCodes` to narrow what counts). The docs' own task: `maxConnections` 1, `http1MaxPendingRequests` 1, `maxRequestsPerConnection` 1 on httpbin; fortio at 2 connections → ~85% 200 / 15% 503, at 3 → ~37% / 63%; proof in the *client* sidecar's `upstream_rq_pending_overflow`. State is per client sidecar.

**Linkerd** (circuit breaking since stable-2.13.0, 2023-04-11): "failure accrual" per client proxy per endpoint; `balancer.linkerd.io/failure-accrual: consecutive` with `consecutive-max-failures` 7, `min-penalty` 1 s, `max-penalty` 1 m, `jitter-ratio` 0.5; probation admits **exactly one real request**; each failed probe backs off exponentially with jitter. 2.20-series docs add a `unified` mode (success-rate threshold 0.8 over 10 s, min 5 requests) that also counts 429 / RESOURCE_EXHAUSTED. Failures: HTTP 5xx and gRPC DATA_LOSS, DEADLINE_EXCEEDED, INTERNAL, PERMISSION_DENIED, UNAVAILABLE. 2.16 (2024-08-13) added HTTPRoute/GRPCRoute retries (`retry.linkerd.io/limit` default 1) and timeouts.

**Cloud and proxies.** AWS ALB target groups: interval 30 s, timeout 5 s, healthy 5, unhealthy 2, and **fail-open** (all-unhealthy → route to all; tunable via `target_group_health.unhealthy_state_routing.minimum_healthy_targets`); NLB similar with fail-open. AWS App Mesh outlier detection (`maxServerErrors`, `interval`, `baseEjectionDuration`, `maxEjectionPercent`, all required) — **end of support 2026-09-30**. ECS Service Connect: fixed outlier detection (≥ 5 failed connections in 30 s → avoid 30–300 s), 2 retries, only timeouts configurable (`perRequestTimeoutSeconds` 15 s, idle 5 min). Azure API Management backend circuit breaker: `circuitBreaker.rules[]` with `failureCondition{count, percentage, interval, statusCodeRanges, errorReasons}`, `tripDuration`, `acceptRetryAfter` (honours a backend `Retry-After`, called out for Azure OpenAI 429s); one rule per backend; approximate across gateway instances; preview 2024-01-22, in the stable 2024-05-01 REST API. GCP backend services: `circuitBreakers` unlimited by default (`maxRetries` 1); `outlierDetection` `consecutiveErrors` 5 but **`interval` 1 s and `maxEjectionPercent` 50%**, `enforcingConsecutiveErrors` 0; decided independently per proxy. NGINX: `max_fails` 1, `fail_timeout` 10 s (window and penalty; ignored for a single-server upstream). HAProxy: `observe layer7`, `error-limit` 10, `on-error fail-check` default. Kong: passive checks are called "circuit breakers", trip-only (only active checks or a manual call re-enable), all counters 0 = disabled. Traefik: expression-driven (`NetworkErrorRatio()`, `ResponseCodeRatio()`, `LatencyAtQuantileMS()`), `checkPeriod` 100 ms, `fallbackDuration` 10 s, and a **recovering** state that ramps traffic linearly over `recoveryDuration` 10 s.

**gRPC.** No application-level breaker; xDS outlier detection (gRFC A50, grpc-go 1.50 / grpc-java 1.49) implements only success-rate and failure-percentage ejection with Envoy's defaults; **grpc-go and grpc-java count any non-OK status as a failure**, client errors included. Retry budget = service-config `retryThrottling { maxTokens (0,1000], tokenRatio }`: −1 per failure, +tokenRatio per success, retries suppressed at ≤ maxTokens/2; `maxAttempts` capped at 5. Defensible breaker classification: count UNAVAILABLE, DEADLINE_EXCEEDED, INTERNAL, UNKNOWN (optionally RESOURCE_EXHAUSTED as overload); ignore INVALID_ARGUMENT, NOT_FOUND, PERMISSION_DENIED, UNAUTHENTICATED, ALREADY_EXISTS, FAILED_PRECONDITION, OUT_OF_RANGE, CANCELLED.

**Kafka / event-driven.** `KafkaConsumer.pause()` / `resume()` per partition keeps group membership while stopping fetches (pause state is not preserved across a rebalance — re-apply it). Spring Kafka `DefaultErrorHandler`: `FixedBackOff(0, 9)` (ten deliveries) then the recoverer; `DeadLetterPublishingRecoverer` → `<topic>-dlt`; `ContainerPausingBackOffHandler` for back-offs longer than `max.poll.interval.ms`. Kafka Connect `errors.tolerance` none (default) | all; `errors.retry.timeout` 0; sink-only DLQ (KIP-298, 2.0.0).

**Classification.** Concurrency-limit breakers: Envoy thresholds + retry budgets, adaptive concurrency, Istio `connectionPool`, GCP `circuitBreakers`, gRPC `retryThrottling`. Failure-trip breakers: Envoy/Istio/GCP/App Mesh outlier detection, Linkerd failure accrual, ECS Service Connect, Azure APIM, NGINX `max_fails`, HAProxy `observe`, Kong passive checks, Traefik middleware, gRPC xDS outlier detection. Health-check style with fail-open: ALB/NLB. **Every one of them keeps state per proxy or instance; none is fleet-coordinated.**

## 4. Operational depth

**Which errors should trip.** Count connection refused/reset, DNS/TLS failures, timeouts, 5xx (especially 502/503/504), gRPC UNAVAILABLE / DEADLINE_EXCEEDED, and slow calls. Do not count caller cancellations (Polly excludes `OperationCanceledException`), 400/404/409/413/422 validation and business errors (Hystrix `HystrixBadRequestException`, pybreaker `exclude`, Finagle response classifiers), gRPC INVALID_ARGUMENT-class codes, or 401/403. Treat 429 and 503 with `Retry-After` (RFC 6585, RFC 9110) as an immediate, bounded open — Azure's "accelerated circuit breaking", Polly's `RetryAfter`, APIM's `acceptRetryAfter`. Latency-based tripping exists only where the library has a slow-call rate (Resilience4j) or where the control is latency-driven (adaptive concurrency); Azure's "inappropriate time-outs" item is the failure mode when it is missing.

**Half-open design.** Single probe (Fowler, Hystrix, Polly v8, Linkerd, opossum, pybreaker default) vs N permitted calls re-evaluated as a batch (Resilience4j 10; Azure "limited number"). Requests arriving during the probe are rejected fast by library breakers and routed around by mesh breakers. Close criteria: batch ratio (Resilience4j), consecutive successes (Azure, pybreaker `success_threshold`, Semian), a single success (Hystrix, Polly, Linkerd, Finagle), or timer expiry with no success requirement (Envoy/Istio). Escalating open duration: Linkerd penalties (exponential, jittered, 1 s → 1 m), Envoy/Istio ejection multiplier (30 s × count, cap 300 s), Polly `BreakDurationGenerator`, Semian `exponential_backoff_error_timeout`, Finagle jittered `markDeadFor` 5–300 s, Azure's manual escalation. Stampede on close: Envoy `max_ejection_time_jitter` exists for exactly this, Linkerd's jitter ratio, Envoy **slow start** (ramp weight over a window), Traefik's recovering state, SRE's cache warming. No controlled experiment isolating the close-time stampede was found (§7).

**Per-instance vs shared state.** Hystrix (2016 maintainer comment), Polly (#215 / #287, closed 2019 in favour of a durable-entity contrib), and Resilience4j (#419 / #2298) all decline distributed counters: a shared store is a new dependency that can fail or be slow, and a failure seen by one node may be local to that node or its path — telling peers to stop destroys the redundancy they provide. Recommended compromise: share only *transition events* (peers call force-open / close), require a quorum, or share per host. Libraries with shared state: pybreaker `CircuitRedisStorage` (fails to `fallback_circuit_state`, closed by default), gobreaker `DistributedCircuitBreaker`. Shopify's Semian: the **bulkhead** is host-wide (SysV semaphores) but breaker state is per worker process; the shared-memory breaker PR (2019) is still open. LiteLLM shares cooldowns through Redis for multi-replica gateways. AWS Prescriptive Guidance's Step Functions + DynamoDB breaker is a deliberately shared-state variant. Granularity: Azure "resource differentiation" and Brooker's shard example → per endpoint / authority (Polly `SelectPipelineByAuthority`, .NET hedging handler), per host (Envoy/Istio ejection), per tenant or API key (LLM quotas).

**Alternatives and complements — when each beats a breaker.** Dependency slower, not down → adaptive concurrency limits. Many small clients with poor per-client estimates → client-side adaptive throttling or a retry token bucket (graceful, not binary). Retry storms → retry budget + single retry layer + jitter (Envoy `retry_budget`, gRPC `retryThrottling`, Finagle `RetryBudget` 20% + 10/s floor, AWS SDK token bucket, Yandex 10%). Own service overloaded → prioritized load shedding (Netflix, Uber, Stripe). Work outlives the caller's patience → deadline propagation and cancellation (gRPC deadlines propagate in Java/Go). Producer/consumer mismatch → backpressure and bounded queues. Dependency hard-down or a fallback genuinely cheaper → the breaker.

**Testing and operating.** Fault injection: Toxiproxy toxics (`latency`, `timeout`, `down`, `bandwidth`, `slow_close`, `reset_peer`, `limit_data`, `slicer`; per-toxic `toxicity` probability), Istio VirtualService `fault.delay` / `fault.abort` with percentages, AWS FIS (`aws:ecs:task-network-latency`, `aws:fis:inject-api-throttle-error`, …), Chaos Mesh HTTPChaos / NetworkChaos, Gremlin blackhole / latency, WireMock faults (`CONNECTION_RESET_BY_PEER`, `EMPTY_RESPONSE`, lognormal delays) for unit tests. Deterministic tests via programmatic transitions (`transitionToOpenStateFor`, Polly `ManualControl`, `StateProvider`). Kill switches: Resilience4j `FORCED_OPEN` / `DISABLED` and the actuator write operation; Polly `IsolateAsync`; Hystrix `forceOpen`. Dashboards: Resilience4j Prometheus series `resilience4j_circuitbreaker_state`, `_failure_rate`, `_slow_call_rate`, `_calls_seconds_count`, `_buffered_calls`, `_not_permitted_calls_total`; Polly v8 meter `Polly` with `resilience.polly.strategy.events`, `.attempt.duration`, `resilience.polly.pipeline.duration` and tags `pipeline.name`, `strategy.name`, `operation.key`. Alert shapes (own formulations on documented functions): open longer than X → `state{state="open"} == 1` held for X; flapping → `changes(state{state="open"}[15m]) > N`. Flapping mitigations with sources: success ratio over N probes, escalating open duration, jitter, minimum call volume before evaluation, asymmetric healthy/unhealthy thresholds, ramped recovery.

**Failure modes of the breaker itself.** Modal behaviour that is untested until the incident (Brooker; Gabrielson's fallback story); partial failure hidden or converted into total failure by wrong granularity (Brooker's shards; Azure); non-idempotent calls executed twice when retry + breaker + async fallback interact — use an idempotency key generated once per business operation and reused across retries, redeliveries and fallback (Stripe stores the first response per key for 24 h; IETF `Idempotency-Key` draft-07: 422 mismatch, 409 concurrent duplicate; RFC 9110 on unsafe methods; .NET `DisableForUnsafeHttpMethods`); breaker + cache staleness (serving cached data while open *is* the fallback mode Amazon warns about, and the HotOS cache-loss loop is its mirror image); stampede on close; a breaker over a shared thread pool that does nothing about the threads already blocked (Azure "inappropriate time-outs").

## 5. Breakers around LLM provider APIs

**Anthropic (official docs + in-session Claude API reference).** Error classes: 400 `invalid_request_error`, 401 `authentication_error`, 402 `billing_error`, 403 `permission_error`, 404 `not_found_error`, 409 `conflict_error`, 413 `request_too_large`, **429 `rate_limit_error`** (per-organization RPM / input-TPM / output-TPM token buckets; carries `retry-after` seconds plus `anthropic-ratelimit-*` limit / remaining / reset headers; a tier spend-cap 429 has **no** `retry-after`), 500 `api_error`, 504 `timeout_error`, **529 `overloaded_error`** (high traffic across all users). Errors can also arrive **mid-stream after a 200** as an `error` event. Refusals are not errors: `stop_reason: "refusal"` arrives with HTTP 200 (do not retry unchanged; modify or fall back); `model_context_window_exceeded` is likewise a 200 stop reason. SDK defaults: `max_retries` 2 on connection errors, 408, 409, 429 and ≥ 500 with exponential backoff (Python: 0.5 s → 8 s cap, ±25% jitter; `retry-after` honoured when ≤ 60 s; `x-should-retry` overrides); default timeout 10 min (TypeScript scales it up to 60 min for large non-streaming `max_tokens`); timeouts are retried, so wall-clock can reach timeout × (retries + 1).

**OpenAI.** 429 variants (rate limit with `Retry-After`; "slow down"; no prepaid credits / `insufficient_quota` — not fixed by retrying), 500, 503 model overloaded; `x-ratelimit-*` headers; SDK `max_retries` 2, 600 s timeout, `retry-after` honoured up to 120 s.

**Gateways and routers.** LiteLLM Router: `allowed_fails` (source default 3), `cooldown_time` (source default 5 s; docs text says a minute), `allowed_fails_policy` per error class, `num_retries` 2, `fallbacks`, `context_window_fallbacks`, `content_policy_fallbacks`; a 429 cools a deployment immediately; most other 4xx never cool it; otherwise cooldown when the per-minute failure share exceeds 50% with ≥ 5 requests; `ContextWindowExceededError` and `ContentPolicyViolationError` never trigger cooldown; cooldowns shared via Redis across replicas. Portkey: retries on 429/500/502/503/504 (one page adds 529) with 1/2/4/8/16 s backoff and `retry-after` honoured to a 60 s cap; circuit breaker `failure_threshold`, `failure_threshold_percentage`, `cooldown_interval` (min 30 s), `failure_status_codes` (default > 500), `minimum_requests`; bypassed when every target is open. Cloudflare AI Gateway: ordered provider fallbacks, `cf-aig-request-timeout` measured to **first byte**, `cf-aig-max-attempts` ≤ 5, `cf-aig-backoff`. Kong AI Proxy Advanced: `failover_criteria` default `["error","timeout"]`, passive `max_fails` 0 (off), `fail_timeout` 10 s.

**What counts for an LLM call.** Count 529, 500/502/503/504, connection failures, per-attempt timeouts, mid-stream error events, slow calls. Special-case 429: honour `retry-after`; it is a per-organization / per-key quota signal, so scope the trip to that key, tenant and model, not the provider; a spend-cap 429 or `insufficient_quota` should open a non-expiring breaker with an alert. Do not count 400 validation / context-length errors, 401/402/403, 413, or content refusals (HTTP 200). **Latency dominates:** with 10-minute default timeouts, an error-count breaker sits closed while every thread waits; set a per-attempt timeout on time-to-first-token and a separate total deadline, prefer streaming so latency is observable, and make slow-call rate an independent trip criterion (Resilience4j's 60 s / 100% defaults are off for this purpose).
